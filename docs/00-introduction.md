# Read This First

## Who this book is for

You have heard that you can run AI on your own computer, and you want a straight answer about how to do it. You do not need to be a programmer. You need to be willing to type a few commands into a window, one at a time, and sometimes read the output before pressing Enter.

That is the whole skill. Everything in this book assumes you are starting from zero.

## What this book covers

Here is the trip map, chapter by chapter:

1. Why running AI on your own computer may be worth your time.
1. What your hardware can and cannot do, with honest RAM numbers.
- How to install Ollama on Windows, macOS, and Linux.
- How to pick small, modest models that do not choke old laptops.
- Your first conversations through the terminal.
- A free desktop chat window that is not a browser tab.
- What the privacy trade-offs actually are, in both directions.
- When local AI beats the cloud, and when it does not.
- A glossary you can keep open in another tab.
- A one-page hardware checklist before you install anything.

## How to use it

Read chapters 2 and 3 before installing anything. After that, the order stops mattering much. Chapters 4 through 6 are the hands-on ones; 7 and 8 are the judgment chapters.

Rules this book follows in every chapter:

- Every command you type is shown in a box, and you can copy it exactly as written.
- Nothing here requires a subscription, an account, or an internet connection after the initial download.
- If a step can fail, the chapter says so and shows what it looks like when it happens.

## The quick start, for the impatient

If you just want to try it in five minutes and your machine has at least 8 GB of RAM:

```bash
ollama run llama3.2
```

That single command includes downloading the Ollama app (chapter 3), downloading the model, and starting a chat. Type a question such as "Explain what offline AI means" and press Enter. Type `/bye` to leave.

If that is enough for you today, close the book guilt-free. Chapter 5 explains the full tour when you come back.

More resources live on the author hub at [https://dylanasdale.github.io](https://dylanasdale.github.io), including companion guides on AI basics and free tools.
